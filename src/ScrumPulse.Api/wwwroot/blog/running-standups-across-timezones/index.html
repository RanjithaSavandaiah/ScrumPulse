What actually works for standups across timezones

Scrum practice19 July 20265 min readRanjitha Savandaiah

A standup works when the people in it can act on what they hear. Split a team across a four or five hour offset and that assumption quietly breaks, because by the time one half hears about a blocker the other half has gone home.

Protect the overlap rather than the ceremony

The scarcest resource in a distributed team is the window where everyone is awake. Most teams spend the first fifteen minutes of it on a status round that could have been read asynchronously.

Inverting that helped more than anything else we tried: updates written down before the call, and the live window spent only on the things that need two people in a room — blockers, decisions, pairing. The meeting got shorter and considerably more useful.

Write the blocker down, with a clock on it

A blocker mentioned aloud at a time when the person who can unblock it is asleep has not been raised in any practical sense. Written blockers with a visible elapsed time survive the handover. The person coming online sees a nineteen-hour-old item, not a sentence in a meeting they missed.

Rotate who is inconvenienced

If the call is always at a time that suits one location, the other location is permanently the junior party regardless of anyone's intent. Rotating the awkward slot costs a little convenience and buys something worth more than the inconvenience.

Keep the timer, and keep it visible

Two minutes each sounds mechanical and it is the single change people thank you for later. It is not about efficiency. A visible, equal allocation means the quietest person gets the same space as the most talkative one, and on a distributed call, where social cues are thin and the person on poor audio simply loses, that equality does not happen on its own.

What did not work

Recording the call for the absent half. Nobody watched them. A twelve-minute recording asks more of someone's morning than a written summary and delivers less, and after three weeks the recordings were being made and not opened by anyone.
